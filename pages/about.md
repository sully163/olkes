## About the Collection

The Cheryl Olkes collection of African art is comprised of over 600 art objects from the African
continent, donated by Chatham University class of '70 alumna Cheryl Olkes. Additionally, the
collection includes a physical copy of her 1989 book co-authored with her former husband
Paul Stoller titled "In Sorcery's Shadow: A Memoir of Apprenticeship Among the Songhay of
Niger." A large majority of the items represented in the collection were created in the late
nineteenth or early twentieth century. However, at the item-level, these objects have very
little known provenance or records pertaining to their creation, so more precise dates are
not discernable. Likewise, the names of specific artists with items present in the collection
have not been recorded and are instead attributed to the culture they were created in. A large
proportion of the collection originates from West and Central Africa; this includes items from
dozens of different African peoples, such as the Dogon, Kuba, Yaka, Tanzanian, Nyamwei,
Songye, Mbole, Suku, Luba, or Bembe peoples. A selection of items from the collection are
believed to be contemporary, "fake" art objects created for the Western tourist market based
on their craftsmanship and materials, but the exact likelihood of this is unclear due to a lack
of documentation. Genres of items within the collection include masks used for masquerades,
sculptural figures, musical instruments, basketry or other textiles, tools and weapons, and
other miscellaneous divination objects. These items are primarily constructed out of analogous
wood (often camwood), bronze or other metals such as gold, shells, and/or plant fibers called
"raffia." Large textiles or other two-dimensional mediums are not present in this collection.
